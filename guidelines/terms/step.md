distinct part of a :term[process] that the user interface presents as a distinct stage in the user’s progress toward completing the process, rather than as an individual interaction within a stage.

:::note
A review stage is a step when it requires reviewing information as part of the process before confirmation or progression (for example, reviewing order details before placing an order). Passive reading or viewing of information is not a review step.
:::

:::note
The following are generally not considered steps:
* success pages or messages presented after completion;
* confirmation receipts;
* thank-you pages or messages;
* completion summaries presented after submission;
* opening a tooltip;
* expanding an accordion when the expanded content does not require action to continue the process;
* displaying contextual help.

However, stages such as success pages, confirmation receipts, thank-you pages, and completion summaries may be considered steps when the author explicitly identifies them as part of the multi-step process (e.g., a thank-you page identified as "Step 3 of 3"). In such cases, they are considered part of the process and must be treated consistently across the process.

Other interactions, such as opening a tooltip, expanding an accordion, or displaying contextual help, are not considered steps unless they require a meaningful action to achieve the intended outcome of the process.
:::
